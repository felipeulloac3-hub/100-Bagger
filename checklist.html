The 100-Bagger Checklist

113 questions drawn from Pabrai, Fisher, Mayer, Akre, Phelps, Lynch, Cassel and Munger — each one attributed to the practitioner it came from, and marked for whether a machine or a human has to answer it.

A checklist is not a screenerPabrai runs his at the end of research, immediately before committing capital — a pre-flight check on a business he already understands, not a net dragged across the market.
Failures are weighed, not countedMost companies fail some questions. A failed leverage question and a failed culture question are not one point each.

Removed or corrected

Draft itemProblem
Dividend yield exactly 0%Not a criterion in any source. Mayer's data includes dividend payers. Demoted to a minor signal.
Cash > total liabilitiesA net-net criterion from a different strategy. XPEL and ACU both failed it — a test every good business fails carries no information.
(CapEx + R&D) / CFO > 80%R&D already sits inside operating cash flow, so this double-counts and rewards cash-burning R&D shops. Replaced with Akre's ROIIC.
Share count flat over 3 yearsContradicts the reinvestment thesis for a young company. Replaced with a dilution-rate test plus Fisher's forward-looking version.
PEG on revenue growthPEG is earnings growth by construction. Using revenue makes margin compression look like cheapness. Mayer's ~1 threshold kept, denominator fixed.
Short interest under 10%Contradicts the document's own sentiment logic, and is noise below $300M.
Asset turnover > 1.0 as "asset-light"Inverted. Genuinely asset-light firms often show low turnover because of large cash and intangible balances.
Net cash as a share of priceA cigar-butt criterion — and the same document correctly notes cigar butts never become 100-baggers.
No price-cutting in transcriptsMost sub-$300M companies hold no earnings calls, so absence auto-passes. Replaced with the margin-evidence test.
Glassdoor scrapingViolates site terms and has no legitimate free API. Fisher #7 is the sourced version, answered by a human.
Market cap under $1B as a gateMayer's median starting cap was ~$500M with 68% under $300M — a strong preference, not a wall.
Equal weight across all 50The sources are explicit that leverage, moat and management account for 70–80% of failures.

On Pabrai's list. He has never published it. What is documented is its method, its category weighting, and a handful of questions he has named in talks. Items credited to him here are either publicly stated or direct operationalizations of a failure mode he has described — not a leaked copy, and anything claiming to be one deserves suspicion.

Not investment advice. A score is not a recommendation. No checklist identifies a 100-bagger — 100x is a multi-decade outcome contingent on execution nobody can forecast. This concentrates attention on the few businesses showing the signature early.
