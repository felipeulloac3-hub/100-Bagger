# Backtest

_As of **2012-12-31**, held to **2022-12-31** (10 years)._

## What this does and does not prove

Facts filed after the as-of date were pruned before scoring, so no 
restatement or unfiled fiscal year leaked in. The universe was built from 
EDGAR frames rather than today's ticker list, so companies that have since 
been delisted are still in it.

The comparison that matters is **flagged versus the rest of the same 
universe**, not flagged versus an index. Beating the S&P with small caps 
proves you bought small caps. Beating the small caps you did not pick is 
the only result attributable to the screen.

## Verdict

**No verdict.** 30% of flagged names and 52% of rejected ones have no price series, a gap of 22 points against a 10% limit. The two groups are not comparable, so the difference in their medians measures which group lost more members rather than which group performed better.

Marking the missing names to a total loss does not rescue it: that hands the win to whichever group kept more members, which is the same artefact with the sign flipped.

The cause is that a forward return needs a ticker, and SEC lists only a company's current one — delisted, acquired and wound-up companies have none. Narrow it by feeding the run more history: `python -m scanner.harvest_tickers` pulls old copies of SEC's ticker file out of the Wayback Machine, and `data/tickers.csv` takes symbols by hand. See the coverage section above for which of the two failures is binding.

The rule-lift analysis in `reports/rule-lift.md` is unaffected, because it compares rules *within* the priced names, where every observation has the same attrition.
